A LITTLE SCIENCE. A LOT OF WONDER.

A tiny symbol.
A cosmic question.

Λ (“lambda”) is Einstein’s cosmological constant: one possible way to describe dark energy. It is not Newton’s gravity constant. The fate of space also depends on its matter, geometry and how dark energy behaves.

Collapse · Λ < 0

Negative vacuum energy can lead an expanding universe to turn around and collapse. Our glowing rebirth is a speculative Big Bounce, not an established prediction. The outside view and central gathering point are artistic storytelling.

Sustain · Λ finely balanced with matter

Imagine gravity and cosmic expansion poised on a tightrope. Einstein once explored a static universe with positive Λ balanced against matter and curvature. That balance is unstable: a tiny disturbance can undo it. This is a “what if”, not a forecast, and Λ = 0 does not automatically mean a static universe.

Expand · constant Λ > 0

If positive dark energy stays constant, expansion can keep accelerating. Faraway galaxies slip beyond view. Bound systems do not simply stretch apart: our local neighbourhood stays together while its stars age. Much later, usable energy becomes scarce—the idea behind heat death.

The Sun first swells into a red giant, sheds its outer layers, and leaves a white dwarf. That stellar story happens on a very different timescale from cosmic isolation and the fading of all stars. Our film joins those chapters into seconds. Its viewpoint is an imagined observer near the Solar System, not a prediction that Earth survives.

We know expansion is accelerating. We do not yet know the final ending.
